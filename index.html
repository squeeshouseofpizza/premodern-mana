How the target numbers are worked out

The requirement numbers come from Frank Karsten's 2022 article, which is the standard reference for this. A mana base consistently casts a spell if you have at least an 89 + M percent chance of drawing the coloured sources you need by turn M, counting only the games where you drew enough lands in the first place. So a one-drop is held to 90 percent, a four-drop to 93 percent. Failing to cast a one-drop on turn one usually costs you a turn; failing to cast your five-drop on turn five often costs you the game, so the bar rises with the curve.

The conditioning matters. These numbers say nothing about whether you drew enough lands, only about whether the lands you drew were the right colours. Land count is a separate question with a separate formula.

The other rule worth knowing: on turn one only untapped sources count. From turn two onward Karsten counts everything, including taplands, on the reasoning that you could have played the tapland earlier. That is exactly why this page shows you two numbers per colour instead of one.

Why not just use an existing calculator

Karsten counts a fetchland as a full source of every colour it could find, because in Modern it finds a shock dual. He flags lands that cannot find a dual as a separate and harder case worth only about two thirds of a source. Premodern is entirely that harder case. There are no dual lands in the format, so Flooded Strand is a Plains or an Island that costs a life. Every general-purpose manabase tool inherits the Modern assumption and none of them know this.

His model also assumes that lands are your only mana sources. Premodern is a ritual format. Dark Ritual, Lotus Petal, Lion's Eye Diamond and Mox Diamond all see play and none of them is a land, so a count that only looks at your mana base is missing real sources in one direction while the point below has it missing them in the other.

Then there is the part no generic tool touches, which is that a Premodern land's mana symbols routinely lie about what it produces. Skycloud Expanse shows white and blue and makes neither one on its own. Ancient Spring's two abilities have no colour in common, so a card that scans as a three-colour land is a blue land with a one-shot escape hatch. Cabal Coffers and Gaea's Cradle read as fixing and produce nothing at all on an empty board. That is what the classification underneath this page is for, and it is why the numbers here will not match what you get pasting the same list into a general-purpose calculator.
